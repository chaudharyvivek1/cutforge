# v2.10 candidates — open findings against v2.9

All six findings from the previous list are resolved in v2.9. What follows is what v2.9 leaves open.

## 1 — No NVENC path and no measured capacity

**Severity: high, and it is the last blocker before v3.0.**

Every number in §12 is a target. `build/benchmark.py` exists and has no runner; the workflow is manual-only because a scheduled job with no runner queues forever. The chunked render now works on a CPU, which means the *logic* is proved and the *throughput* is not.

**Proposed resolution.** A GPU machine, one benchmark run, and the matrix filled in. This is procurement, not engineering — the code is written and waiting.

## 2 — The chunked render is proved on raw frames, not on a file

**Severity: medium.**

The v2.9 test asserts that concatenated raw frames equal a single pass. That is the right assertion for the filter graph and it says nothing about the encoded file: two encodes of the same frames with different GOP boundaries are different bytes, and §11.5's promise is about what a customer receives.

**Proposed resolution.** Encode each segment with a closed GOP and concatenate at the container level, then compare decoded frames rather than encoded bytes. That is what a real chunked renderer does, and it is where the seam problems live.

## 3 — Nothing produces a transcript

**Severity: medium, unchanged in shape since v2.8.**

`RecordedTranscriber` replays a fixture. There is no ASR adapter and no audio analysis, so §10.4's word-level timings have never come from audio.

**Proposed resolution.** One adapter over any hosted ASR, tested against a recorded response. The remap and the contract are proved; only the producer is missing.

## 4 — Three GPU signals remain unemitted

**Severity: medium, blocked.** Unchanged since v2.6.

## 5 — Segment publishing has no eviction or lifecycle

**Severity: medium, and v2.9 created it.**

Segments are published as derivatives and counted against the storage quota, and nothing ever removes them. A resumed render leaves its segments forever, so a tenant's storage grows with every render attempt — which is a cost bug wearing a cache's clothes.

**Proposed resolution.** A retention rule: segments expire after the render completes, or after N days for a resumable render. One column and one sweep, and it needs to exist before the quota matters.

## 6 — The identity corpus still has no data

**Severity: medium, unchanged for six versions.** Procurement.

---

## How this list is maintained

A finding enters when it is noticed and substantiated — a section number, an arithmetic error, or two rules that cannot both hold. It leaves when a version resolves it, and the resolution is named in that version's §0 table. It is never quietly dropped.

Two of the six are now blocked on hardware rather than on work, which is the first time the list has been gated by procurement rather than by design. That is what the roadmap predicted, and it is the signal that the specification has outrun the implementation.
