# v1.5 candidates — open findings against v1.4

All ten findings from the previous list are resolved in v1.4. Its §0 table has twelve rows because two of them split into an arithmetic error and the invariant that hid it. What follows is what v1.4 leaves open, including three items that v1.4 created by resolving something else — a resolution is a change, and every change has edges.

Open findings live here rather than in the specification, because a document that lists its own unresolved defects teaches a reader to distrust every claim in it. The spec stays firm; the debt stays countable.

## 1 — The VFR half of the conform map is specified but unproven

**Severity: high. It is the one part of v1.4's central mechanism with no worked example.**

§5.9 gives two implementations of the conform map: `affine` for constant-rate sources and `table` for VFR. Appendix A exercises the affine path thoroughly — five clips, four rates, every conformed span matching its record duration. The table path has no fixture at all.

The risk is specific. The affine path has an algebraic inverse, so the round-trip property in FR-15 can be reasoned about. The table path cannot: its inverse is a lookup of `source_pts`, and its monotonicity depends on the ingest having sorted the packet timestamps. A VFR file with out-of-order PTS, or with a gap larger than the nominal interval, can produce a map that is non-decreasing but not strictly increasing — which is legal and which no test currently exercises.

**Proposed resolution.** A VFR fixture in the §19 corpus: a phone screen recording with a PTS spread beyond 1% of nominal, plus one deliberately reordered packet stream. Assert monotonicity, assert `|toConformed(toNative(c)) − c| ≤ 1` for every conformed frame, and add a row to Appendix A showing the table path beside the affine one so the two are read together.

## 2 — The atempo cascade has no fixture at an awkward ratio

**Severity: medium.**

§5.11 states that speeds outside 0.5–2.0 compile to a cascade generated from the exact rational, with the residual tracked after each stage. The rule is right; the example is friendly. `4×` becomes `atempo=2.0,atempo=2.0` exactly, which proves nothing about residual handling.

A speed of 3.7 needs `2.0 × 1.85`, and 1.85 is not representable in the filter's parameter space without rounding. Whether the compiler passes `1.85` as a decimal, a fraction, or a sample-accurate ratio determines whether the output is one sample long or one sample short over a three-minute clip.

**Proposed resolution.** Two fixtures at ratios that do not decompose into powers of two — 3.7× and 1/3.7 — asserting the emitted sample count against `samplesFor(R)` exactly. Add a §5.11 sentence stating the precision at which the residual is passed to atempo, because "tracked" is not a number.

## 3 — Erasure quarantine has no owner and no release path

**Severity: high, and it is a product decision before it is a legal one.**

§16.6 quarantines rather than deletes, and says explicitly that retention is a legal decision. That is the right boundary. What it does not say is who can release a quarantined render, what happens to a tenant who quarantines their only copy of a deliverable, or whether a quarantined render still counts against storage quota.

Left unanswered, quarantine becomes a state that things enter and never leave — which is worse than deletion for the person whose data it is, because the data still exists.

**Proposed resolution.** Add a release path with three named outcomes: `withdrawn` (render deleted, delivery revoked), `retained_lawful` (render kept, serving stopped, retention justified and logged), and `restored` (quarantine lifted after the erasure was found to be in error). Each needs an actor and a deadline, and the erasure log needs a terminal state so the count of unresolved quarantines is visible in §17.

## 4 — `origin.params_hash` is undefined per engine

**Severity: medium.**

§6.8 adds `origin: {engine, job_id, version, params_hash}` and states that two jobs with the same hash are interchangeable. That claim only holds if every engine hashes the same kind of thing. The 3D engine's parameters are a scene graph; the dubbing engine's are a voice profile and a language; a generative engine's are a prompt and a seed.

A shared field name with three different definitions is a field that will be compared across engines and found equal when it is not.

**Proposed resolution.** Each engine publishes its own `params_hash` definition in its contract document, and the video engine treats the hash as opaque — comparable only within one `engine` value. That is one sentence in the contract and one line of validation: a hash comparison across differing `engine` values is rejected rather than silently trusted.

## 5 — A transition beside a sped clip has no handle rule

**Severity: medium.**

§5.7 requires `T/2` frames of handle on each side of a centred dissolve, and §5.9.3 puts speed arithmetic in conformed space. Neither says what a handle is when the clip next to the transition runs at 2×.

The handle is measured in record frames, so the source frames it consumes depend on the speed: a 12-frame dissolve needing 6 record frames of handle consumes 3 source frames at 2× and 12 at 0.5×. A compiler that checks handle availability in source frames will pass a clip that has no handle at all.

**Proposed resolution.** State the rule in record frames, and derive the source requirement through the same transform as §5.10. Add a fixture with a dissolve against a 2× clip whose handle exists in record space and does not exist in source space.

## 6 — The 540p preview proxy is required but not costed

**Severity: low.**

§11.3 requires a second proxy at 540p because it brings preview iteration from 0.599× to 0.224× pixel work (§12.5). §20 accounts for render and planning, and §20.2 lists storage as excluded.

So the decision that makes iteration affordable adds a storage and ingest cost that no figure includes. At 4K60 that is not a rounding error: a second proxy is roughly a third of the 1080p proxy's bytes over the same footage.

**Proposed resolution.** Add the proxy pair to §12.7's storage table as one line, and to §20.2's excluded costs as a named driver rather than a category. The point is not precision; it is that a required artefact should appear in the cost model that motivated it.

## 7 — Beat snapping from a sped music bed is undefined

**Severity: low.**

§5.10 says a tempo map derived from a music asset is projected through that asset's conform map like any other annotation. It does not say what happens when the music clip itself carries a speed.

A 124 BPM track played at 1.05× is 130.2 BPM, and the beat grid must scale with it. Because §5.10's transform divides by speed, the projection is already correct for positions — but the *interval* between beats changes, and the snap tolerance is authored in milliseconds against a tempo that no longer applies.

**Proposed resolution.** State that a tempo map is stored in source time and projected through the full transform including speed, so both positions and intervals scale together. One fixture: a beat-snapped edit over a clip at 1.05×, asserting the resulting cut positions against a hand-computed grid.

## 8 — Storage and egress remain outside the cost model

**Severity: medium, and it grows with every 4K60 customer.**

§20.2 names storage, egress, database, vector index, idle capacity and analysis GPU as excluded, with their drivers. That is honest, and it was honest in v1.2 as well. What has changed is that §12.11 now presents a reconciled cost per output minute — a figure that reads as complete and is not.

At 4K60 an hour of source is 20–45 GB and a deliverable is 18 GB per hour. For a platform storing originals for a retention period and serving outputs to viewers, storage and egress are plausibly larger than render compute.

**Proposed resolution.** Either fold storage and egress into the §20 table with their own drivers and a stated retention assumption, or rename the table to "compute and token cost per output minute" so no reader mistakes it for the whole. The second is a one-line fix and is the honest minimum.

## How this list is maintained

A finding enters when it is **noticed and substantiated** — a section number, an arithmetic error, or two rules that cannot both hold. It leaves when a version resolves it, and the resolution is named in that version's §0 table. It is never quietly dropped.

Three of the eight above were created by v1.4's own resolutions: the VFR path by the conform map, the handle rule by putting speed in conformed space, and the beat interval by projecting tempo maps through the transform. That is normal and worth stating plainly — a resolution that produces no new edge is usually a resolution that did not change anything.
