## 1 to 4 — the four purchases, fifth version running

Unchanged, and no version can move them. The repetition is now the most useful thing this list contains: five consecutive versions have ended with the same four items, which is the definition of a treadmill.

## 5 — The calibration set is still written by one person

**Severity: medium, and v2.14 sharpened it.**

Twenty plans, one author, and that author is also the author of the rubric. v2.14's two disagreements are evidence the set is not entirely circular — one of them corrected the rubric rather than the label — but a set written by the rubric's author will drift toward the rubric's blind spots.

**Proposed resolution.** Someone else labels twenty plans from the same prompts without seeing the scores. Every disagreement is then either a rubric change or a labelling rule, and the exercise is only honest if it is sometimes the first.

## 6 — The quality gate has no way to refuse

**Severity: low, and v2.14 created it.**

The gate repairs and reports. If the repair cannot reach an acceptable score — an empty plan, a plan whose shots are all one frame — the job is still accepted and will fail later, in the renderer, where the error is more expensive and less legible.

**Proposed resolution.** A 422 with the criterion that could not be repaired, before the job is queued. §15.1 already has an error envelope for exactly this.

## The recommendation

**Stop cutting versions.** Two of six findings are worth a session; four are worth a purchase. A v2.15 that closed 5 and 6 would produce a v2.16 list with nothing on it but the same four purchases, which is where v2.12, v2.13 and v2.14 have all arrived.

What the next session should be:

1. **A GPU quote or a rented hour.** Closes findings 1 and 2 and fills §12's matrix with real numbers.
2. **A model budget.** Closes finding 3 and produces the first plan a model has ever made against a calibrated rubric.
3. **A corpus contract.** Closes finding 4 and starts the weeks of lead time it needs.

Until one of those exists, the repository is at the useful end of what a specification can do alone: 335 tests, twenty-four reviews, no known contradiction, and every remaining claim labelled as a target rather than dressed as a result.
