## 5 — The blind judge still has one author

**Severity: medium, and v2.15 made it honest rather than solving it.**

`label_from_request()` writes the expectations before a plan exists, so a label can no longer be revised after seeing a score. That removes the *mechanical* circularity. It does not remove the human one: the expectations themselves are still written by the person who wrote the rubric, and a shared blind spot survives the exercise.

**Proposed resolution.** A second person writes `accept_if` for the same twenty requests without seeing the plans or the scores. Comparing two independently written expectation lists is a better test of the rubric than comparing a rubric to its own author.

## 6 — The two judges agree because they read one set of numbers

**Severity: low, and it is the tension v2.15 chose deliberately.**

v2.15's four corrections all consisted of making the judge read the rubric's constants. That removed the disagreements and, with them, a source of independent evidence: two judges agreeing on every plan is now partly true by construction.

The alternative — two judges with independent rules — produced seven disagreements of which most were bugs and one was a missing criterion, which is more informative and much noisier. There is no third option that is both independent and cheap, and the honest statement is that agreement is now a regression guard rather than a validation.

**Proposed resolution.** Keep the shared constants for the automated gate, and add the second author from finding 5 as the independent check. Independence belongs in a person, not in a second copy of a constant.
