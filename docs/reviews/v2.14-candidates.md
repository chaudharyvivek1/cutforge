# v2.14 candidates — open findings against v2.13

Six findings closed in v2.13. What it leaves open is the shortest list yet, and the first time it contains nothing that a version can fix alone.

## 1, 2, 3 and 4 — the four purchases

Unchanged for a fourth consecutive version, and the repetition is now the finding: **the specification has outrun what this environment can prove.** No further version can move any of these, and three of the four are gated on money rather than on effort.

The honest recommendation is to stop cutting versions and spend the next session on a GPU quote, a model budget and a corpus contract.

## 5 — Ten plans is a small calibration set

**Severity: medium, and v2.13 created it.**

The rubric separates twenty-one pairs perfectly on ten plans, which is enough to show the criteria are not arbitrary and not enough to trust the thresholds. Two of the ten labels had to be corrected during calibration, which means the labelling rule itself is not yet stable — a set that changes when the rubric looks at it is not an independent set.

**Proposed resolution.** Twenty plans the owner labels without seeing the rubric's scores, and a report of where the two disagree. Every disagreement is either a label to fix or a criterion to change, and the exercise is only useful if the answer is sometimes the second.

## 6 — The repair loop is not wired into the job service

**Severity: low.**

`apply_quality_repair()` is called from tests and from nowhere else. The service submits a plan and renders it; the quality repair is a library function a caller must remember.

**Proposed resolution.** Call it in the plan job between validation and render, and record the diff on the job so a repaired plan is visible rather than silent.

---

## How this list is maintained

A finding enters when it is noticed and substantiated — a section number, an arithmetic error, or two rules that cannot both hold. It leaves when a version resolves it, and the resolution is named in that version's §0 table. It is never quietly dropped.

**This list is the argument for stopping.** Two findings are worth a version; four are worth a purchase. Continuing to cut versions would close findings 5 and 6 and produce a v2.15 list with nothing on it but the four purchases again — which is a treadmill, and naming it is more useful than riding it.
