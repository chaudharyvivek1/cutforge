## 1 and 2 — the GPU findings

**Severity: high and medium; both blocked on the same thing.**

`build/benchmark.py` is written, the workflow is manual-only, and no runner labelled `gpu` exists. Every number in §12 is a target. Three of §17's nine signals cannot be emitted by a CPU worker.

Nothing in this repository changes that. Both findings stay open until a machine exists, and the honest thing is to say so rather than to emit zeros or to schedule another version that cannot close them.

## 3 — The model path has no implementation at all

**Severity: high, and it is now the largest remaining piece of work.**

§7.1 specifies two passes over a language model. `tests/planner.py` implements the deterministic template planner that P0 ships *first*, and `golden/plan_corpus.json` records its plans. Nothing has ever called a model.

This is the difference between "the engine can assemble an edit from a structured request" and "the engine can assemble an edit from a sentence", which is the product.

**Proposed resolution.** One adapter over any model, a recorded-response fixture per pass, and the two-pass plumbing. The evaluation corpus and the 80% target come after; the plumbing does not need a budget.

## 4 — Identity accuracy has a harness and no corpus

**Severity: medium, unchanged for seven versions.** Procurement. `tests/corpus.py` is the specification of what to buy.

## 5 — The chunked render has never run on a real timeline

**Severity: medium, and v2.10 narrowed it rather than closing it.**

The tests chunk two synthetic 24-frame clips. Appendix A's timeline — five clips, four transitions, mixed rates — has been compiled and rendered whole, but never split. Its transitions are exactly what `split_at_cuts` exists to avoid cutting, so the fixture is the one that would prove the rule.

**Proposed resolution.** Render Appendix A whole, then chunked, and assert the decoded frames match. That is the test the current one is a stand-in for.

## 6 — Segment expiry has no policy, only a mechanism

**Severity: low.**

`expire_segments(older_than_seconds)` works and nothing calls it on a schedule, and no retention period has been chosen. A mechanism without a policy is a function nobody runs.

**Proposed resolution.** Pick a retention — the render's lifetime plus an hour is the obvious default — and call the sweep from the worker's idle path.

## How this list is maintained

A finding enters when it is noticed and substantiated — a section number, an arithmetic error, or two rules that cannot both hold. It leaves when a version resolves it, and the resolution is named in that version's §0 table. It is never quietly dropped.

**Three of six are now gated outside the repository** — hardware, a model budget and procurement. That is a different state from every previous list and it is worth stating plainly: the specification has outrun what this environment can prove, and the next real progress is a purchase rather than a commit.
