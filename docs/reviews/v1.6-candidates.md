# v1.6 candidates — open findings against v1.5

All eight findings from the previous list are resolved in v1.5 and recorded in its §0 table. What follows is what v1.5 leaves open.

Two of the six below are **verification debt rather than specification defects**: the rule is stated correctly and nothing yet exercises it. They are listed here because the difference matters — a rule that is wrong needs a version, and a rule that is unexercised needs a fixture.

## 1 — Audio transition handles on a sped clip are unstated

**Severity: high.**

v1.5 fixed the *picture* half of this: a transition's handle is measured in record frames and the source requirement is derived through the speed. The audio half is untouched, and it fails the same way one layer down.

An `acrossfade` of T frames consumes T/2 frames of audio from each side, which is `samplesFor(T/2)` samples — and if the adjacent clip runs at 2×, the source samples needed are twice that. §5.11 asserts that source samples consumed equal `samplesFor(C)` for the clip as a whole; a transition overlapping the clip's head changes where that clip starts consuming, and nothing states the rule for the overlap.

**Proposed resolution.** State that audio transition handles follow the same rule as picture handles — measured in record frames, converted to samples through `samplesFor`, multiplied by speed for the source requirement — and add a fixture with an `acrossfade` against a 2× clip whose source audio is exactly long enough in one domain and not the other.

## 2 — A table-backed map with speed is walked non-uniformly

**Severity: high.**

§5.9.4 proves the table map on a still clip. §5.10's transform divides by speed. Put them together and a subtlety appears: for an affine map, dividing by speed is a uniform stretch, so the walk through source frames is even. For a table map it is not — a VFR clip at 2× must visit every second *conformed* frame, and the conformed frames are themselves irregular in source time, so the interval between visited source frames varies.

The arithmetic still terminates, because the map is total and monotone, but nothing states whether speed is applied before or after the table lookup, and the two orders give different source frames for the same record frame.

**Proposed resolution.** State the order explicitly: record frame → divide by speed → conformed frame → table lookup → source timestamp. Then a worked example on the §5.9.4 excerpt at 2×, showing the visited source frames and asserting the sample count.

## 3 — `render_duration_frames` and `include_trailing_audio` overlap

**Severity: medium.**

v1.3 split one duration into three to fix a contradiction, and added `include_trailing_audio` as a boolean. v1.4 kept both. Two fields now describe the same decision: a render duration longer than the content duration *means* trailing audio is included, and the boolean means it too.

Two ways to say one thing is the defect v1.2 was criticised for. Either the boolean is derived from the durations and should not be stored, or the render duration is derived from the boolean plus the tracks and should not be stored.

**Proposed resolution.** Keep `render_duration_frames` as the authoritative field, since verification and the frame count depend on it, and make `include_trailing_audio` a derived convenience in the API response only — never an input. State the derivation and add an invariant that the two agree.

## 4 — Erasure deadlines are asserted without a jurisdictional basis

**Severity: high, and it is a legal question the document should not answer by itself.**

§16.6.1 sets 30 days for withdrawal, 90 for a lawful-retention review and "before the erasure's own deadline" for restoration. Those numbers are plausible and unsourced. India's DPDP Act, 2023 and its rules set their own expectations, and comparable regimes differ; a deadline in a technical specification reads as a commitment.

**Proposed resolution.** Keep the mechanism and remove the pretence of authority: state the deadlines as configurable defaults with the note that counsel sets them per jurisdiction, and add a `jurisdiction` field to the erasure record so the deadline is a function of one. This is the same treatment §16.3 gives consent, and it should be consistent.

## 5 — The VFR corpus fixture is specified and not built

**Severity: medium, verification debt rather than a defect.**

§5.9.4 names the fixture — a 60 s phone screen recording with a PTS spread beyond 1% of nominal, plus one stream with reordered packets — and §19 asserts the properties. No such file exists, so the properties are asserted against nothing.

**Proposed resolution.** Generate the fixture rather than sourcing it: ffmpeg can produce a VFR stream from a CFR source with `-vsync vfr` and a select filter, and packet reordering can be synthesised. A generated fixture is reproducible in CI, which a captured screen recording is not.

## 6 — Egress is priced at one flat rate

**Severity: low.**

§20.3 multiplies 18 GB by nine cents. Real egress is tiered, cheaper at volume, free between some regions, and materially different behind a CDN with a 95th-percentile commitment. The structural point — that delivery dominates compute — survives any of those, which is why this is low severity.

**Proposed resolution.** Replace the single rate with a two-tier illustration and a sentence naming the CDN commitment as the variable that actually sets the price. Do not attempt a model; the point is the order of magnitude and the shape.

## How this list is maintained

A finding enters when it is noticed and substantiated — a section number, an arithmetic error, or two rules that cannot both hold. It leaves when a version resolves it, and the resolution is named in that version's §0 table. It is never quietly dropped.

The taxonomy in `skills/technical-spec-versioning/references/finding-taxonomy.md` classifies these: §1 and §2 are domain mismatches (the class that has produced a finding in every version since v1.1), §3 is count drift, §4 is an unenforceable policy, §5 is verification debt and §6 is evidence presented as fact.
